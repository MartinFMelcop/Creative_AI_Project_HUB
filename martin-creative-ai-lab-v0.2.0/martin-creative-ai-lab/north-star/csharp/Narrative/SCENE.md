# The coil and the shutters — experimental authored scene

**Status:** original English prototype fiction by Codex under Martin's portfolio
production direction. Rill, Edda, the coil and the shutter task are experimental
names and circumstances, not approved North Star canon. No P04/P10 characters,
places, dialogue or fictional continuity are imported. The running consumer, not
this document, owns available commands, arithmetic and observed outcomes.

## Beneficiary and dramatic question

The player has already been able to prepare and survive encounters; here a person
can refuse a request, offer bounded help and react to what actually happened. Can
the player deliver a replacement coil across a surveyed route while Edda still
needs five shutter crates secured before dark? Rill's time cannot be assigned as
cargo. Helping one task leaves less time or capacity for the other.

This is an enacted local request, not a global alignment system. Refusal carries
no moral score or permanent character penalty. Agreement earns no relationship
points. A promised return, actual return and completed delivery are separate facts.

## Playable beats and authored intent

| Beat | Action and visible consequence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Request | Edda sets down a coil beside five shutter crates. Four shifts remain. Rill keeps the guide line secured: the far route needs an actual survey first. | Opening a request cannot assign Rill, deliver the coil or consume daylight. |
| Survey | The player executes the existing first Crossing encounter and spends one shift. | A selected intention is not a completed survey. A failed encounter does not become evidence of a safe route. |
| Fresh offer | After that survey, the player hears Rill's current offer. Edda still needs the remaining crates handled. | Hearing earlier words or merely choosing a guide cannot supply current permission. |
| Tentative agreement | Offer, dusk/open terms and explicit confirmation remain separate steps. The current local cost is visible before confirmation. | Nothing leaves while the proposal is tentative; reservation removes Rill from local work only after confirmation. Changed local work requires a fresh consultation. |
| Choice in action | Local work moves two crates per shift while Rill is available, one while reserved for the coil or away. Guided departure uses two shifts; solo departure uses three and does not lend Rill. Both require the actual second Crossing encounter. | Travel time differs; the combat rules do not secretly become easier for agreement. |
| Withdrawal or refusal | Before departure, withdrawing restores Rill locally and retains the withdrawn receipt. Deferral makes no promise. Declining keeps local work available. | No one is compelled to volunteer; no elapsed travel time is refunded after departure. |
| Account | Edda's response distinguishes coil delivery, Rill's observed return, daylight and crates still unsecured. | Dusk is not an automatic return. A failed attempt permits the player’s retreat to local work but leaves Rill unaccounted for; it does not assert death, rescue, successful delivery or a completed-journey return. |

The arithmetic deliberately retains a cost: survey plus guided travel leaves only
one local-work shift; a solo journey uses all four shifts including survey. Local
work can be prioritized instead. There is no demonstrated perfect outcome that
finishes both jobs in this bounded day. The fixed values are an experimental
situation, not validated economy, travel simulation or player preference.

## Voice and staging for implementation review

Edda should handle a work object rather than announce a theme. Rill should secure,
prepare, release or reclaim the guide line when permission changes. Reservation
for preparation must not be described as an observed departure. Their responses should
name a current fact, not grant generic praise. For example, a tentative coil offer
must leave the line secured; a refusal should let Rill return to local work rather
than condemn the player. An unsuccessful journey must leave the delivery entry
unchecked. These are editorial acceptance criteria, not claims of animation or
human comprehension. Inspect the actual terminal transcript for what is rendered.

## Limits and next creative acceptance

Execution can establish transition legality and truthful displayed consequences.
Agent reading can assess the intended causal chain; neither establishes reader
understanding, emotional force, accessibility, consent comprehension, literary
quality or fun. This local terminal scene is not Unity rendering, a Windows player,
Steam delivery, multiplayer public reputation or persistent quest migration. Future
work should inspect actual authored interaction and player observation rather than
inflate these two people into an unsupported world simulation.
